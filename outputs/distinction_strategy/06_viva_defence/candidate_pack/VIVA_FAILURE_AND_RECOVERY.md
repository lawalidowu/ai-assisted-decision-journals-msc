# Failure and recovery language

Encourage clarification, evidence reference, correction, bounded uncertainty. **Do not bluff.**

## Not remembering an exact number

“I don’t want to invent the figure — the frozen journal / evidence map records it as … I’ll open that reference.”

## Not understanding a question

“Could I clarify whether you mean mechanical traceability or journal validity? They’re separated in this study.”

## Examiner challenges an assumption

“That’s fair. The dissertation treats that as a limitation: [limit]. The claim we do make is only [bounded claim].”

## Possible inconsistency raised

“If I’ve been imprecise, the authoritative path is [file/table]. Let me reconcile against that rather than from memory.”

## Demo fails

“The presentation argument is already complete on the slide. Fallback is `demo/print.html` or the JSON under `demo/evidence/`.”

## Slide skipped / time cut

“I’ll jump to the centrepiece and contribution: quote support ≠ journal membership — then limitations.”

## Answer too long

“Stopping there — the short version is [one sentence]. Happy to expand on evidence if useful.”

## Earlier answer was imprecise

“I should correct my earlier wording: [precise]. The data show [X]; they do not show [Y].”

## Asked for significance / CI not in study

“We did not report confidence intervals or significance tests for that comparison; I won’t invent them. What we report is [exact agreement / κ / counts].”

## Pressed to say it generalises

“I can’t claim generalisation beyond this case study. Future work would be cross-inquiry and multi-model replication.”
