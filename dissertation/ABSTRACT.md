# Abstract

Important decisions made during public emergencies are often difficult to reconstruct because the supporting evidence is dispersed across complex public records. Public inquiry transcripts illustrate the problem: substantive decisions appear alongside questions, procedure, advocacy and retrospective narrative. This dissertation examines AI-assisted decision journaling as a method for creating and reviewing traceable candidate entries from such records, using UK COVID-19 Inquiry Module 2 hearing transcripts as a case study.

Eight hearing transcripts produced 414 candidate entries with mechanical source-quote traceability checks. The candidates were stored as a fixed reference dataset and reviewed through non-destructive flags. A stratified sample of 50 entries was assessed separately for journal validity and evidence strength, and automated confidence signals were compared with those judgements. Exploratory clustering organised all 414 candidates for navigation. A separate purposive sample of 60 entries tested Joint External Evaluation preparedness mapping, Decision Quality mapping and candidate-statement faithfulness through source-based human adjudication.

Of the 414 candidates, 351 passed mechanical traceability. In the stratified n = 50 sample, the most frequent validity-by-evidence cell was no × high (21/50): the quotation strongly supported the generated statement, but the item did not meet the journal definition. Automated confidence aligned more closely with evidence strength than journal validity. In the purposive n = 60 pilot, an observable Decision Quality element was identified more often than a defensible JEE mapping (37/60 versus 11/60), while generated wording was not consistently faithful enough to serve as authoritative evidence.

The findings support keeping candidate generation separate from evaluation and treating validated source passages as authoritative. The main contribution is a governed extraction-and-review process that may support source-linked retrospective review and organisational learning. The conclusions remain bounded by the eight-transcript case study and single-reviewer assessments; stronger independent human validation should precede wider scaling.
